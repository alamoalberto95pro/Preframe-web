There are two ways to pack for a shoot. The first is to stand in front of everything you own and ask "what if I need this?" — and the answer is always yes, so it all goes in the bag. You carry eleven kilos up a hill and use a body and one lens.

The second is to ask a different question: *which of my planned shots needs this?* If the answer is "none", it stays home.

I travel with a Sony a7 II and a Tamron 20–40mm, an action camera, a 360 camera and my phone. That's already five things that film, and on a trip with no plan I'll carry all five every day and use two. The gear isn't the problem. Not knowing what each piece is *for* is.

## Gear belongs to shots, not to bags

A packing list written on its own is a list of fears. A packing list derived from a shot list is a list of facts.

So the gear decision moves earlier — into the shot itself. When you plan a shot, you write what it's filmed on:

| Shot | Type | Camera | Lens / mount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old town from the bridge | Wide, static | Main camera | 20–40mm, tripod |
| Walking through the market | Follow, handheld | Main camera | 20–40mm |
| Tram passing, low angle | Detail | Action camera | Mini tripod |
| The square from the centre | 360, reframed later | 360 camera | Monopod |
| Coffee, hands | Detail | Main camera | 20–40mm |

It takes ten seconds a shot, and you were going to decide it anyway — just later, on location, with the light going.

## Then count

Turn the shot list sideways. For each piece of gear, how many shots use it?

| Gear | Shots that need it | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Main camera + 20–40mm | 31 | Goes |
| Tripod | 9 | Goes |
| Action camera | 6 | Goes |
| 360 camera + monopod | 2 | Hmm |
| Telephoto | 0 | Stays home |
| Gimbal | 1 | Hmm |

Three verdicts fall out immediately:

- **Zero shots: it stays.** No discussion. This is the row that takes two kilos off your back.
- **Many shots: it goes**, and you think about a spare battery for it, because a lot depends on it.
- **One or two shots: decide on purpose.** Either the shot is worth carrying the thing for — sometimes it is; one 360 shot at the peak of the video can be the shot — or you redesign the shot for gear you're already carrying. A gimbal move for one shot often becomes a careful handheld walk.

## Pack by day, not by trip

If the shoot is more than a day, do the count per day. The 360 camera has two shots and both are on day three: it lives in the hotel until then. The tripod's nine shots are all dawn and dusk: it comes out for those and stays behind at midday.

Light decides this as much as location does, which is why it pays to [plan each shot around its light window]({{ langBase }}/guides/plan-shots-around-the-light/) first and pack second.

## What the count doesn't cover

The list from your shots is the *creative* kit. Add the boring layer that every shoot needs regardless: batteries and a way to charge them, cards and a way to back them up, a cloth, rain cover, the right plates and cables. Those don't depend on any shot, so write them once and reuse the same block every time.

## The same thing in PreFrame

In PreFrame, gear is part of every shot. The shot editor has a camera and a lens — brand and model — next to the shot's type, duration and notes.

You don't retype your kit each time. Your cameras and lenses live in a personal inventory that travels with you across projects, so assigning gear is picking from what you own. And a shot pulled from your [shot library]({{ langBase }}/guides/reusable-shot-library/) arrives with the gear it worked on already attached.

Once it's on the shot, the gear shows wherever the shot does: on its card in the section, and on every shot of the printed shooting plan.

The count is still yours to do — read down the plan and tally — but it's a two-minute job, because every shot already says what it needs. If a piece of gear never appears in the plan, you have your answer before you've opened the bag.

Carry what the video needs. Leave what your nerves want.
