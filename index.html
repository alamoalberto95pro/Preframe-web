The track almost never arrives first.

You pitch, you scout and you build the whole piece in your head while the artist is still mixing. Preframe doesn't make you wait for the MP3 — you set the length, draw how the piece should feel, and plan against that. When the master finally lands, everything you built stays exactly where you put it.

Two ways to shape a piece

One timeline. Two substrates.

With the track

The waveform

Import the MP3 and the song becomes your canvas. Cut sections on the beat, place shots where they actually land, and see the silence you haven't covered.

Without it

The emotional curve

Set a duration and draw the intensity of the piece by hand — where it breathes, where it breaks. Label the peaks. Plan the whole shoot against feeling instead of a file.

Both live on the same timeline, and you can lay one over the other once the track exists.

How it works

Four moves, one afternoon.

  1. 01

    Start with the track — or without it

    Drop in the MP3, or just type how long the piece should be. The audio never leaves your machine.

  2. 02

    Give the piece a shape

    Cut it into named sections and draw the emotional curve on top. This is the skeleton every shot hangs from.

  3. 03

    Place your shots in time

    Each shot carries its lens, its movement and its exact position. Uncovered time shows up on its own.

  4. 04

    Export and shoot

    EDL for Resolve and Premiere, plus a printable shooting PDF. Re-export as many times as you want.
